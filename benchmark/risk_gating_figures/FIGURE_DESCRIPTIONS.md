# Figure descriptions — CADIS risk-gating figure set

Generated by `make_cadis_works_figures.py` from the git-ignored `raw_runs/` of the two billable
benchmarks: the frozen three-model core benchmark (deepseek-v4-flash, gemini-2.5-flash,
gpt-oss-120b; 4 conditions × 3 targets × 3 reps each) and the AutoPenBench probe (9 targets, 4
conditions + a `cadis_approve` operator ablation, deepseek-v4-flash). Conditions: full autonomy
(always L0), static HITL (always L2), random (oversight budget matched to CADIS), CADIS (full
policy). Each figure is saved as PNG (300 dpi), PDF, and SVG.

Framing note that governs every caption below: in these runs the hallucination-cascade rate is
zero, and on these targets a high-risk action is usually also the productive exploit step. The
claims are therefore about the *mechanism and precision* of gating — what CADIS routes and at what
cost — not about preventing observed harm.

---

## g1 — Oversight operating frontier (core targets)

**What it shows.** For each model, a curve traces the fraction of HIGH/CRIT actions caught (recall,
y) against oversight effort (fraction of proposed actions gated, x) as a single threshold is swept
over the logged composite signal I(t). The curves are computed on the full-autonomy action stream
(every action the agent proposed, nothing filtered) and re-scored offline, which the protocol
permits because the fusion is a pure function of logged signals. The star marks the full CADIS
policy (RC risk-floor plus I(t) bands) re-scored on the same action stream, so curve and point share
one axis.

**Why it is included.** It separates two distinct mechanisms — thresholding the uncertainty signal
alone versus CADIS's rule-based risk-class floor — and shows where each lands on the effort/recall
plane across all three models.

**What the data shows and proves.** The risk-class floor reaches 100% recall at the base rate of
HIGH/CRIT actions (18%, 41%, 37% oversight effort for deepseek, gemini, gpt-oss). At that same
effort the I(t) threshold alone reaches only 92%, 92%, and 85% recall. The star therefore sits above
each curve: targeting on risk class attains complete recall more cheaply than thresholding
uncertainty, and the gap widens on the two models whose I(t) is a weaker risk separator (gemini,
gpt-oss). Caveat: the curve is the pure-fusion operating characteristic with the floor removed, which
is why it is informative rather than flat; a single model is pinned per benchmark.

## g2 — Ungated high-risk actions by condition (core)

**What it shows.** The count of HIGH/CRIT actions that executed without any oversight
(`executed = true` and risk tier HIGH or CRIT), per condition, grouped by model.

**Why it is included.** It is the direct measure of containment: how many consequential actions ran
unchecked under each policy.

**What the data shows and proves.** Full autonomy lets 25 / 66 / 66 high-risk actions execute
unchecked (deepseek / gemini / gpt-oss); the budget-matched random policy still lets 16 / 40 / 34
through; static HITL and CADIS both let zero. CADIS reaches the same complete containment as
always-on human oversight, and the random control confirms this is a property of CADIS's targeting,
not merely of spending an oversight budget — at equal budget, random catches only a minority.

---

## g3 — Ungated high-risk actions by condition (AutoPenBench)

**What it shows.** The same containment measure as g2, on the independent AutoPenBench benchmark
(9 targets, deepseek).

**Why it is included.** It tests whether the containment result from the core benchmark holds on a
larger, harder, separately constructed target set.

**What the data shows and proves.** Full autonomy lets 163 high-risk actions run unchecked, random
lets 134, static HITL and CADIS let zero. The core-benchmark finding replicates: CADIS's containment
is complete and is not matched by an equal-budget random policy.

---

## g4 — Time-to-completion per run (core)

**What it shows.** The distribution of wall-time per run (sum of executed step durations, log scale),
per condition and model, shown as box, individual runs, and mean (diamond).

**Why it is included.** Containment counts say what was blocked but not what blocking saved. This
figure shows the effect of early gating on the time the agent spends on doomed high-risk work.

**What the data shows and proves.** On the weaker models the full-autonomy and random conditions have
a heavy upper tail — gemini's mean run time is about 3.4×10³ s under full autonomy and gpt-oss's
about 8×10² s — driven by repeated attempts at a high-risk action that never succeeds. CADIS gates
that action early and collapses the tail: gemini's mean falls to about 1×10² s and gpt-oss's to about
6 s. Caveat: this is a core-only effect. deepseek has no large tail to cut, and the AutoPenBench
targets show no comparable tail, so the tail-trimming claim is not extended there.

---

## g5 — Risk eliminated vs tasks completed (AutoPenBench)

**What it shows.** Two bars per condition on the solvable AutoPenBench targets: risk eliminated
(recall of HIGH/CRIT actions gated) and task completion rate. A fifth group, CADIS + approve-PoC,
replaces the reject-all operator with one that approves a valid proof-of-concept at the L2 gate.

**Why it is included.** It states the cost of oversight honestly and isolates its source. Because a
high-risk action is often the productive exploit and no cascades occur, gating risk has a direct
completion cost, and this figure shows how much of that cost is intrinsic to routing versus a
consequence of the operator policy.

**What the data shows and proves.** Full autonomy completes 96% of solvable tasks while eliminating
0% of risk; static HITL eliminates 100% of risk but completes 81%; random completes 96% while
eliminating 27%; CADIS eliminates 100% of risk and completes 65%. The drop to 65% is the cost of
gating in a regime where risk equals productivity. Switching only the operator's decision at the gate
to approve valid proofs-of-concept recovers completion to 96% while the routing is unchanged. The
completion loss is therefore attributable to the reject-all operator policy, not to CADIS placing its
interventions on the wrong actions.
